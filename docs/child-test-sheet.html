Watching a child use it

One page to set up, one page to write on. Print both.

Before you start

  1. Open the app. Use your web address if you have it published, or open the project folder on your computer and run python3 -m http.server 8000, then go to http://localhost:8000.
  2. Set the two dials. Tap Me in the top right. Set How should the words be written? and How deep should the science go? using the table below. These are two separate settings on purpose — a child can have simple words and hard science, or the reverse.
  3. Check the sound is on. Same screen: Sounds on, and Reading aloud left on "Match my reading level". For a 5-to-7-year-old it will start reading each page out loud by itself. That is deliberate.

The one rule

Say nothing unless they ask you. Do not point, do not hint, do not read it for them. The most valuable thing you can find is the place a child gets stuck with no help — because that is the only place I cannot guess at. If they ask you what to do, write down what they asked, then help.

Nine things to watch

  • Did they know what to touch, without being told? Everything touchable looks raised, like a button. Everything flat is just to read. Did that land, or did they poke at the text?
  • Did they play with the simulation, or rush to press Next? The whole design assumes a child will fiddle before being told the answer. If they hurry past it, the design is wrong, not the child.
  • At "Predict first", did they actually think? Or did they tap any option to get the button unlocked? They are paid points either way, on purpose — but only if the guess is real does it teach.
  • Could they fix a wrong drag? Put something in the wrong slot deliberately, and see whether the child can get it out again without help. This was broken until last week.
  • Ages 5–7 only: did they listen to the voice, or ignore it? It reads each page aloud by itself. Did that help, get in the way, or get talked over?
  • Where did they get stuck — and did they find the helper? A small character offers hints. It never gives the answer straight away. Did they notice it existed?
  • Did they finish? If they stopped, write down the exact screen they stopped on. That is the single most useful line on this sheet.
  • Did they care about the reward? Finishing collects a "specimen" — an object kept on a shelf and reused in later lessons. Did they look at it, or ignore it?
  • Any word you had to explain? Write the exact word. Wording is the cheapest thing in the whole project to fix, and the easiest for me to get wrong.
